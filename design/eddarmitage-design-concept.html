Cutting GitHub Actions minutes with self-hosted runners

Our CI bill crept up quietly over a year until it wasn't quiet anymore. This is what I tried, in order, and what actually moved the needle — job gating, caching, trigger changes, and eventually a self-hosted runner on a Proxmox LXC.

Contents
  1. The problem
  2. Cheap wins first
  3. Self-hosted runners on Proxmox
  4. Was it worth it

The problem

Minutes usage had roughly tripled since the previous year, mostly from integration tests running on every push rather than every merge.1 Nothing in the pipeline was wrong exactly — it was just running far more often than it needed to.

01 — This is usually the first thing worth checking before touching infrastructure at all: are you running the expensive job too often, not just too slowly?

The obvious first move is gating: only run the full suite on pushes to main or when a PR is marked ready for review, and run a fast subset everywhere else.

↑ Back to contents

Cheap wins first

Before reaching for self-hosted runners, three changes bought real headroom with no new infrastructure to maintain:

on:
  push:
    branches: [main]
  pull_request:
    types: [ready_for_review]

Combined with dependency caching, this cut minutes usage by roughly 40% before any hardware was involved.

02 — Caching node_modules or the OpenTofu provider cache is often the single biggest win per line of YAML changed.
↑ Back to contents

Self-hosted runners on Proxmox

For the jobs that were still eating minutes, an LXC container on the existing NUC runs a self-hosted runner that picks up the heavier integration jobs. It's not a fit for every job — anything that needs a clean, disposable environment stays on hosted runners.

↑ Back to contents

Was it worth it

For this repo, yes — mostly because the hardware already existed. If it meant buying a machine specifically for this, the cheap wins alone would probably have been enough.

Weeknight chana masala

Serves 3–4 · 30 minutes · one pan

Ingredients

  • 2 tins chickpeas
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 1 tin chopped tomatoes
  • Garam masala, cumin, turmeric

Method

  1. Soften onion and garlic in oil, 5 minutes.
  2. Add spices, cook 1 minute until fragrant.
  3. Add tomatoes and chickpeas, simmer 15 minutes.
  4. Mash a few chickpeas to thicken. Season and serve.
